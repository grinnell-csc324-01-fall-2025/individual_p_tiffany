## 1. Research Questions

1. Can users successfully complete basic tasks (e.g., inputting mood, viewing feedback), 
and is the interface intuitive?    

2. Do users find the AI’s text feedback helpful, easy to understand, and credible for a therapy-like context?    

3. Do users find the emotion tracking feature (logging/reviewing moods across the month) useful for self-reflection?   

## 2. Lo-fi Prototypes

- Exp1: Simple Figma wireframes or static HTML screens with input boxes, buttons, and navigation.  
- Exp2: Predefined “mock AI responses” instead of real model output.  
- Exp3: Mockup of a simple calendar or chart view for emotions.  

## 3. Experiment Playbook

### Experiment 1: 
- **Script**:  
  Ask participants to complete:  
  1. Enter today’s mood  
  2. Click the “Get Feedback” button  
  3. View the result/summary  
- **Rules**: No external instructions.  
- **Data Collected**: Task completion time, number of errors, verbal feedback.  

### Experiment 2: 
- **Script**:  
  Provide scenario: *“Today I feel very stressed about exams.”*  
  Show 2–3 pre-written AI responses (e.g., “It’s normal to feel anxious during exams…”).  
- **Rules**: Users will evaluate responses based on personal preferences.  
- **Data Collected**: Users will be asked to rate AI responses on several factors using a scale from 1 to 5,  
with 5 being very satisfactory:  
  1. Sincerity  
  2. Effectiveness in pinpointing the feelings described  
  3. Effectiveness in providing advice and comfort  

### Experiment 3: Emotion Tracking Value
- **Script**:  
  Show mockup of an “emotion calendar” of a weekly summary.  
  Ask participants to log moods for the past week and check summary.  
- **Rules**: No instructions in navigating. Users will evaluate based on pesonal preferences.  
- **Data Collected**: User reflections on self-awareness and their improvement suggestions.  

## 4. Takeaways

### Experiment 1
Participants consistently felt that the plain white background was too monotonous and impersonal.  They explained that it does not create a comfortable atmosphere for confiding emotions. In addition, when every module is simply stacked vertically, the overall structure feels awkward to navigate. They suggested adding a left-side toolbar for better organization and a more pleasant user experience. Most participants were able to complete the tasks within one minute.  

### Experiment 2
My friends thought that the current prototype feels overly simplistic and somewhat cold. Many of the responses were only a single line, and the wording sounded very “AI-like.” Compared to interacting with a real friend, the sense of closeness was missing. For ratings, they suggested giving scores of **3, 2, and 0** across different factors. They noted that while AI often comes across as polite and formal, it lacks the warmth and human touch needed to make conversations feel personal.  

### Experiment 3
Participants appreciated the mood-recording feature, but felt it did not differ much from keeping a regular diary. They suggested adding more detailed labels, such as noting how many small unpleasant events occurred in a day. They also wanted the option to customize and add their own labels to each day’s record, making the feature more meaningful. In addition, they recommended a “compare” button that would allow them to select and compare two different days, or compare today with a past day. This, they believed, would help them better track their personal growth and transformation.
