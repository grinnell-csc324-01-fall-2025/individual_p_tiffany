## 1. Research Questions

1. Can users successfully complete basic tasks (e.g., inputting mood, viewing feedback), 
and is the interface intuitive?    

2. Do users find the AI’s text feedback helpful, easy to understand, and credible for a therapy-like context?    

3. Do users find the emotion tracking feature (logging/reviewing moods across the month) useful for self-reflection?   

## 2. Lo-fi Prototypes

- Exp1: Simple Figma wireframes or static HTML screens with input boxes, buttons, and navigation.  
- Exp2: Predefined “mock AI responses” instead of real model output.  
- Exp3: Mockup of a simple calendar or chart view for emotions.  

## 3. Experiment Playbook

### Experiment 1: 
- **Script**:  
  Ask participants to complete:  
  1. Enter today’s mood  
  2. Click the “Get Feedback” button  
  3. View the result/summary  
- **Rules**: No external instructions.  
- **Data Collected**: Task completion time, number of errors, verbal feedback.  

### Experiment 2: 
- **Script**:  
  Provide scenario: *“Today I feel very stressed about exams.”*  
  Show 2–3 pre-written AI responses (e.g., “It’s normal to feel anxious during exams…”).  
- **Rules**: Users will evaluate responses based on personal preferences.  
- **Data Collected**: Users will be asked to rate AI responses on several factors using a scale from 1 to 5,  
with 5 being very satisfactory:  
  1. Sincerity  
  2. Effectiveness in pinpointing the feelings described  
  3. Effectiveness in providing advice and comfort  

### Experiment 3: Emotion Tracking Value
- **Script**:  
  Show mockup of an “emotion calendar” of a weekly summary.  
  Ask participants to log moods for the past week and check summary.  
- **Rules**: No instructions in navigating. Users will evaluate based on pesonal preferences.  
- **Data Collected**: User reflections on self-awareness and their improvement suggestions.  

## 4. Takeaways

- Experiment 1:
Participants consistently felt that the plain white background was too monotonous and impersonal.  
They said it doesn’t create a comfortable atmosphere for confiding emotions.  
In addition, if every module is simply stacked vertically, the overall structure feels uncomfortable to navigate.  
They suggested that a left-side toolbar would provide better organization and a more pleasant user experience. Tasks are completed within a minute.

- Experiment 2:
My friends think that the current prototype feels a bit too simplistic and somewhat cold.  
Many of the responses are just a single line, and the wording sounds very “AI-like.”  
Compared to interacting with a real friend, the sense of closeness is missing.  
For ratings, they would like to give 3, 2, 0.  
They feel that AI always comes across as polite and formal, but it lacks the warmth and human touch that makes conversations feel personal.

- Experiment 3:
My participants quite like the mood-recording feature, but they feel that it doesn’t differ much from keeping a regular diary.  
Beyond just recording moods, they suggested adding more detailed labels, for example noting how many small unpleasant events occurred in a day.  
They would also like the option to customize and add their own labels to each day’s record, which would make the experience more meaningful.  
In addition, they hope for a “compare” button that allows them to select and compare two different days, or today versus a past day.  
This could help them better see their personal growth and transformation.
